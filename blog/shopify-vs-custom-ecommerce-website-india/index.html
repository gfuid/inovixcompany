Shopify vs Custom Ecommerce Website in India: Fees, Control and Scale (2026)

Rent a shop in a mall, or build your own showroom? That is the Shopify vs custom decision in one line. Here are the details.
Sagar Punia
Founder & Senior Web Engineer at Inovix Technologies • Panipat & NCR

The Shopify vs custom ecommerce website question comes up in almost every conversation we have with sellers. Shopify is like renting a shop in a well-run mall: everything works from day one, and you pay rent every month. A custom store is like building your own showroom: more money and time at the start, then it is yours. Neither is "better" in general. This guide shows which one fits which seller.

Shopify's plans and fees change from time to time and differ by country. The figures here are approximate. Check Shopify's official India pricing before you decide.

2. The Real Fee Math on Shopify

Shopify's cost has three parts, and most new sellers only notice the first.

  • Plan fee: the basic plan in India is roughly in the ₹2,000 per month range, with higher plans costing several times more.
  • Transaction fee: Indian stores typically use a third-party gateway such as Razorpay or PayU. Shopify usually charges its own percentage on those orders, around 2% on the basic plan and lower on higher plans, in addition to what the gateway charges.
  • Apps: reviews, COD verification, WhatsApp notifications, bundles, GST invoices, upsells. Many are paid monthly, often priced in dollars. Five or six apps can cost more than the plan itself.

An illustration

Take a store selling ₹3,00,000 a month. These are example numbers, not a quote:

  • Plan: about ₹2,000
  • Shopify transaction fee at about 2%: ₹6,000
  • Paid apps: say ₹3,000 – ₹6,000
  • Total platform cost: roughly ₹11,000 – ₹14,000 a month, or ₹1.3 – ₹1.7 lakh a year, before the gateway's own charges

At ₹50,000 monthly sales the same math is small and Shopify is clearly worth it. At ₹10 lakh monthly sales the percentage fee becomes a serious amount, and moving to a higher plan or a custom store starts to make financial sense.

3. Cost of a Custom Ecommerce Store

A custom store built on React or Next.js with a database, admin panel and payment gateway typically ranges from ₹60,000 to ₹5,00,000 in the Indian market, depending on features. After that you pay for:

  • Hosting, usually modest for small and mid-size stores
  • Payment gateway charges, same as any store
  • Maintenance, either monthly or as needed
  • New features, when you want them

There is no platform percentage on your sales and no per-app rent. But you carry the responsibility: if the developer disappears and you do not have the code, you are stuck. 4. Control and Flexibility

Shopify handles the standard store very well: product, cart, checkout, order. Trouble starts when your business is not standard.

  • Wholesale and dealer pricing: different prices per customer group, minimum order quantities, credit terms.
  • Made-to-order products: custom sizes, fabric choices, price calculated from measurements.
  • Enquiry-first selling: common for exporters and B2B, where the customer requests a quote instead of paying online.
  • Checkout changes: deeper checkout customisation is restricted to Shopify's highest plans.
  • Connecting to your own billing or ERP: possible on Shopify through its API, but you pay for the app or the development anyway.

With a custom store all of this is simply built in. If your selling process has rules that a standard theme cannot express, custom saves you from stacking app upon app.

5. Scale and Performance

To be fair to Shopify: scaling is its strength. It handles sale-day traffic spikes without you thinking about servers, and large brands run on it. If your worry is "will the site crash during a festival sale", Shopify is the safer answer.

A custom store on modern hosting such as Vercel or Cloudflare also scales well, but it has to be built properly, and someone has to watch it. Custom wins on scale in a different sense: your costs do not rise as a percentage of sales, and you can shape the store around operations as you grow.

6. When Shopify Wins

  • You are launching a new brand and need to test demand quickly.
  • Your catalogue and checkout are standard.
  • You have no developer you trust for the long term.
  • You run frequent ad campaigns and want proven apps for reviews, upsells and abandoned carts.
  • Monthly sales are still small, so percentage fees are small too.

7. When a Custom Store Wins

  • You sell B2B, wholesale or made-to-order, with pricing rules a theme cannot handle.
  • Your sales are steady and platform fees have become a noticeable monthly bill.
  • You want the store connected to billing, inventory or a CRM in one system.
  • You want a distinctive brand experience, not a theme many others also use.
  • You want to own the asset outright.

8. A Sensible Middle Path

Many sellers do well by starting on Shopify, proving the product, and moving to custom once monthly sales and requirements justify it. Your products, customers and orders can be exported, so the move is manageable if planned. Others, especially local sellers, start with a simple catalogue site and WhatsApp ordering before investing in either.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is Shopify good for small businesses in India?
Yes, for new and small sellers with standard products Shopify is a strong choice. It launches quickly, handles hosting and security, and has apps for most needs. Just budget for the monthly plan, paid apps and the extra transaction fee on third-party gateways.
Q: Does Shopify charge transaction fees in India?
When you use a third-party payment gateway, which is typical for Indian stores, Shopify generally charges its own transaction fee in addition to the gateway's charges. It is around 2 percent on the basic plan and lower on higher plans. Confirm the current rate on Shopify's official pricing page.
Q: When should I move from Shopify to a custom ecommerce website?
Consider moving when your monthly platform and app fees are consistently high, when you need pricing or checkout logic Shopify cannot handle without many apps, or when you want the store tightly connected to your own billing, inventory or CRM system.
Q: Who owns the store on Shopify versus a custom website?
On Shopify you own your products, customer data and domain, but the platform and theme framework belong to Shopify and the store stops if you stop paying. With a custom site you can own the full source code and move hosting whenever you like, provided your contract says so.
