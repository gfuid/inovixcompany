Restaurant Website Design with QR Menu Ordering System: 2026 Guide for Owners

A plain-language guide for restaurant, cafe and cloud-kitchen owners who want more direct orders and less dependence on food delivery apps.
Sagar Punia
Founder & Senior Web Engineer at Inovix Technologies • Panipat & NCR

Food delivery apps bring orders, but they also take a large cut and keep your customer's phone number with them. That is why more owners are now asking for a restaurant website design with QR menu ordering system: one setup that works as your online identity, your digital menu on every table, and your own direct ordering channel. This guide explains how it works, what it should include and what it typically costs in 2026.

1. What a restaurant website with QR menu ordering system actually is

It is not three different products. It is one web application with three faces:

  • Public website: menu, photos, timings, location, table booking or party enquiry.
  • QR table ordering: each table has a QR code. The guest scans it, sees the menu, and places the order from their phone. No app download.
  • Direct online ordering: customers at home order from your own link for pickup or your own delivery, and pay by UPI or cash.

Behind it is a simple dashboard for the counter or kitchen where new orders appear with a sound alert.

2. How QR table ordering works, step by step

  1. Guest sits down and scans the QR on the table. The table number is already inside the QR.
  2. The menu opens in the phone browser with photos, veg/non-veg marks and prices.
  3. Guest adds items and confirms. They can add notes such as "less spicy".
  4. The order reaches the kitchen screen or printer with the table number.
  5. Guest can add more items later; everything joins the same bill.
  6. Bill is paid at the counter or by UPI.

Your waiters are not replaced. They get more time for serving and for guests who prefer to order by speaking. During rush hours, fewer orders are missed or written down wrong.

3. Direct online orders without aggregator commission

Aggregator apps typically charge a commission on every order, plus you pay for visibility and discounts. For many small restaurants this takes a serious share of the bill value. You should check your own latest statement to see the exact figure.

A direct ordering link changes the maths for your repeat customers. People who already love your food do not need an app to discover you. Put your ordering link in your WhatsApp status, on the bill, on the parcel sticker and in your Google Business Profile. Offer a small benefit such as a free drink for direct orders. Every order that shifts is an order with no commission.

Be realistic: aggregators are still useful for new customer discovery and for delivery riders. The aim is balance, not to quit them overnight.

4. Must-have features for restaurant website design

  • Fast mobile menu with categories, search and clear photos. Slow menus kill orders.
  • Easy menu editing: change price or mark an item "out of stock" from your phone in seconds.
  • Order alerts: sound on the dashboard and, if you want, a WhatsApp alert to the owner.
  • 1-click WhatsApp button for party orders, catering and table booking enquiries.
  • Kitchen order ticket view or printing.
  • Daily sales summary so you know your top items and slow hours.
  • GST-ready bill format if you are registered.

Optional later: loyalty points, coupon codes, multiple outlets, staff logins with role-based access, and inventory.

5. Google Maps presence: where hungry customers search

"Restaurants near me" and "best paneer tikka in Panipat" are searched on Google Maps every day. Your Google Business Profile is often seen before your website. Make sure it has:

  • Correct name, category, timings and phone number.
  • Fresh photos of food and seating, added regularly.
  • Your website and direct ordering link.
  • Replies to reviews, both good and bad.

7. Common mistakes restaurant owners make

  • PDF menu behind a QR. Guests have to zoom and scroll, and they still cannot order. Use a proper mobile menu.
  • No one updates the menu. An out-of-stock item ordered online makes an angry customer.
  • Weak Wi-Fi or network in the dining area. Test scanning from every table.
  • Not telling customers. A direct ordering link nobody knows about gets no orders. Print it everywhere.
  • Buying everything at once. Start with menu and ordering; add loyalty and inventory after the staff is comfortable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do customers need to download an app to order through the QR menu?
No. The QR code opens the menu in the phone's normal browser. The guest selects items and places the order without installing anything.
Q: Can I stop using food delivery apps after getting my own ordering website?
You can, but most restaurants keep both. Aggregators help new customers find you, while your own website is best for repeat customers because there is no platform commission and you keep the customer's contact.
Q: How much does a restaurant website with QR ordering cost?
A simple website with digital menu and WhatsApp ordering button starts at ₹4,999 with Inovix. A full QR table ordering system with kitchen dashboard and billing is custom software starting at ₹49,999+. Hosting and care is ₹500 per month.
Q: Can I change menu prices and items myself?
Yes. A proper system gives you a simple admin panel where you can edit prices, add items and mark dishes out of stock from your phone.
