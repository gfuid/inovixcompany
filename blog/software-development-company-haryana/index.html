Software Development Company in Haryana: Custom ERP, CRM and Portals — Build or Buy?

Custom software is not always the answer. Here is how to decide between building and buying, and what to demand if you build.
Sagar Punia
Founder & Senior Web Engineer at Inovix Technologies • Panipat & NCR

A lot of Haryana businesses run on a mix of Tally, Excel sheets, registers and WhatsApp groups. It works until it does not: stock does not match, a dealer disputes a balance, a follow-up is forgotten. That is usually when owners start looking for a software development company in Haryana. But custom software is an investment, and sometimes a ready-made product is the better decision. This guide helps you decide, and shows what is typically built for the industries found across the state.

1. Build or buy? Decide this first

QuestionBuy ready-madeBuild custom
Is your process standard?Yes — plain accounting, GST billing, payrollNo — your pricing, production or approval flow is unusual
How many tools do staff juggle?One or two, working fineFour or five, with data typed again and again
Monthly per-user feesSmall team, fees are manageableMany users or dealers, fees keep growing every year
Need to connect WhatsApp, machines, website?Not reallyYes, and the ready product does not allow it
How fast do you need it?This weekYou can give a few weeks for a proper build

Our honest advice: keep standard accounting in a standard tool. Build custom only around the part of your business that is different, and connect the two. That keeps cost down and still fixes the real pain.

2. Factory ERP for manufacturing units

Faridabad, Bahadurgarh, Kundli, Bawal and Panipat are full of small and mid-size manufacturers. Big ERP products are often too heavy and too costly for a unit with 30–150 people. A light custom ERP usually covers:

  • Order to dispatch tracking: order received, production stage, quality check, packed, dispatched.
  • Raw material and finished goods stock, with low-stock alerts.
  • Job work and vendor tracking, common in textile and auto-component units.
  • Owner dashboard on mobile: today's production, pending orders, outstanding payments.

The key is to follow how your floor actually works. A Panipat handloom exporter tracking designs and lots needs different screens from a Rewari auto-parts unit tracking batches and rejections.

3. Dealer and distributor portals

Plywood from Yamunanagar, scientific instruments from Ambala, steel products from Hisar, FMCG and agri inputs from Karnal: all move through dealer networks. A dealer portal lets each dealer log in, see their own price list, place orders, check dispatch status and download invoices and ledger. Your team stops answering the same "maal kab niklega" call twenty times a day, and order mistakes drop because nothing is copied by hand from WhatsApp.

4. School, college and hospital systems

Rohtak, Sonipat, Hisar and Kurukshetra are education and medical hubs. For institutes, the common modules are admissions, fee collection with reminders, attendance, exams and a parent login. For clinics and hospitals: appointments, patient records, billing and lab reports.

These systems hold personal and health data, so ask any developer how access is controlled, where data is stored and how backups work. India's data protection law puts responsibility on the organisation collecting the data, so this is not a small point. In many cases a good ready-made school or hospital product is enough; go custom when you have several branches or a process the products do not handle.

5. Custom CRM for sales teams

Real estate offices in Gurugram and Sonipat, coaching centres, solar installers, machinery sellers: anyone with many enquiries and a long follow-up needs a CRM. A custom CRM built for your steps can capture leads from website, ads and WhatsApp automatically, assign them to staff, send reminders and show the owner who followed up and who did not. More detail is in our guide to custom CRM for small businesses.

6. SaaS MVPs for founders

Gurugram has an active startup scene, and founders in other cities are building too. If you have a software product idea, the first version should do one job well for one type of user, with login, payments and an admin panel. Launch in weeks, get paying users, then expand. Spending a year building everything before a single customer has paid is the usual way MVPs fail.

8. Checklist before you sign with anyone

  1. A written scope listing every module and user role.
  2. A demo of working software every sprint, not only at the end.
  3. Source code and database handed to you, in your own accounts.
  4. Clear cost for support and changes after launch.
  5. A plan for moving your old Excel or Tally data into the new system.

Q: Should I build custom ERP or buy ready-made software?
Buy ready-made when your process is standard, such as accounting or GST billing. Build custom when your workflow is unusual, staff juggle many tools, per-user fees keep rising, or you need integrations a product does not allow.
